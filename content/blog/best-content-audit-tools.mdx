## What Is a Content Audit?

A **content audit** is a systematic review of all the content on your website. The goal is to evaluate what's performing, what's declining, what needs updating, and what should be removed.

A typical content audit answers:
- Which pages drive organic traffic and which don't?
- Which posts lost traffic in the last 3-6 months (content decay)?
- Which content is outdated, thin, or duplicated?
- What topics do competitors cover that you don't (content gaps)?

Content audits used to be done manually with Google Search Console exports and spreadsheets. That still works (we cover the manual method below), but tools have automated most of the process.

### When Should You Do a Content Audit?

Most SEO professionals recommend auditing your content at least once every 6 months. For sites publishing 10+ posts per month, quarterly audits catch decay earlier. The alternative is continuous monitoring, where a tool like SerpVive or Frase tracks content health automatically and alerts you when something declines.

## 10. Manual GSC + Spreadsheet

**Best for:** Zero-budget operations or one-time audits.

**What it does:** You export data from Google Search Console, import it into a spreadsheet, and manually compare periods to identify declining pages.

**How to do it:**

1. Open GSC Performance report
2. Set date comparison (last 3 months vs previous 3 months)
3. Go to Pages tab
4. Export all data to CSV
5. Sort by clicks difference (descending)
6. Flag pages with 20%+ decline
7. For each flagged page, investigate queries, check competitors, and plan refreshes

**Cost:** Free (your time only)

**Pros:**
- Completely free
- Full control over the data and analysis
- No tool dependency
- Good way to learn GSC deeply

**Cons:**
- Takes 2-4 hours per site per month
- No automation (you repeat this process every time)
- No diagnosis of why content is declining
- No competitor analysis (you manually check SERPs)
- Easy to miss patterns that automated tools catch
- Data goes stale quickly (weekly manual checks are exhausting)

**Bottom line:** The manual approach works, and we recommend it for anyone just starting with content auditing. It teaches you how to read GSC data and builds intuition. But as your site grows past 100 pages, the manual effort becomes unsustainable. That's when automated tools earn their price. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best free content audit tool?
Google Search Console combined with a spreadsheet is the most thorough free method. SerpVive also offers a free plan with monitoring for 1 site and 100 pages. Screaming Frog's free version crawls up to 500 URLs for technical content auditing.

### How often should you audit your content?
For most sites, a full audit every 6 months catches significant issues. Sites publishing frequently (10+ posts/month) benefit from quarterly audits. Automated monitoring tools like SerpVive and Frase check content health daily or weekly, removing the need for periodic manual audits.

### What's the difference between a content audit tool and an SEO tool?
SEO tools (like Semrush and Ahrefs) cover everything: keywords, backlinks, rank tracking, site audits, and more. Content audit tools focus specifically on evaluating your existing content library. Some tools (like Semrush) include content auditing as one feature among many. Others (like SerpVive) focus exclusively on it.

### Can AI detect content decay?
Yes. Tools like SerpVive use AI (Claude Opus) to analyze why content is losing traffic by comparing your page against current top-ranking competitors. Frase's Content Opportunities feature also uses automated detection. The advantage of AI-powered tools is that they can identify specific causes (outdated data, missing topics, intent shifts) rather than just flagging declining numbers.

### How much does a content audit cost?
It depends on the approach. Manual audits using GSC are free but take 2-4 hours per site. Tool-based audits range from free (SerpVive free plan, Screaming Frog free) to $29-$499/month depending on the tool and plan. Hiring an SEO consultant for a content audit typically costs $500-3,000.
